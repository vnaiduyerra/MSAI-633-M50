# Cricket Rules — Laws of Cricket (MCC)

Source: https://www.lords.org/mcc/the-laws-of-cricket

## Law 1 — The Players

A cricket team consists of eleven players, including a captain. Substitutes may field but may not bat, bowl or act as captain. A player who leaves the field may resume play with the permission of the umpire.

## Law 2 — The Umpires

Two umpires are appointed for a match. The umpires are the sole judges of fair and unfair play. Umpires make decisions on dismissals, runs, wides, no-balls, boundaries and other matters.

## Law 3 — The Scorers

Two scorers shall be appointed to record all runs scored, all wickets taken and, where appropriate, number of overs bowled.

## Law 4 — The Ball

The ball shall weigh not less than 5.5 ounces (155.9 g) and not more than 163 g. The circumference shall be between 8.81 in (22.4 cm) and 9 in (22.9 cm).

## Law 5 — The Bat

The bat shall not exceed 38 in (96.52 cm) in length. The blade shall not exceed 4.25 in (10.8 cm) in width.

## Law 6 — The Pitch

The pitch is a rectangular area 22 yards (20.12 m) in length and 10 ft (3.05 m) in width. The wickets are set 22 yards apart.

## Law 7 — The Wickets

Three stumps, each 28 in (71.12 cm) high, are set in the ground with two bails on top. The stumps are positioned 9 in (22.86 cm) apart.

## Law 8 — The Creases

Creases are painted lines defining the batsman's ground and the bowler's delivery area. The popping crease is 4 ft (1.22 m) in front of the bowling crease.

## Law 9 — Preparation and Maintenance of the Playing Area

The pitch shall be rolled at the start of the innings. The pitch is the central strip between the two wickets.

## Law 10 — Covering the Pitch

The pitch shall be covered to protect it from rain and dew. Covering is mandatory in all first-class cricket.

## Law 11 — Intervals

There are intervals between innings and for meals. The interval between innings shall not exceed 10 minutes. Lunch and tea intervals are typically 40 and 20 minutes respectively.

## Law 12 — Start of Play; Cessation of Play

The match starts with the toss. Play ceases when the ball is declared dead or at the scheduled close of play.

## Law 13 — Innings

Each team bats once in a limited-overs match. In Test cricket, each team bats twice (two innings). An innings is closed when all batsmen are out or the allocated overs are completed.

## Law 14 — The Follow-On

In a two-innings match, if the team batting second trails by 200 or more runs, the captain of the first team may enforce the follow-on, requiring the opposing team to bat again immediately.

## Law 15 — Declaration and Forfeiture

The captain of the team batting may declare its innings closed at any time. A captain may forfeit its innings.

## Law 16 — The Result

The side that scores the most runs wins the match. If scores are equal, the match is a tie. A draw occurs when time runs out before a result is achieved.

## Law 17 — The Over

An over consists of six legal deliveries bowled from one end. A wide or no-ball does not count as one of the six deliveries.

## Law 18 — Scoring Runs

A run is scored when both batsmen cross each other while running between the wickets. Boundary runs of 4 (ball crosses boundary touching ground) or 6 (ball crosses boundary on the full) are awarded.

## Law 19 — Boundaries

If the ball crosses the boundary after touching the ground, 4 runs are scored. If it crosses without touching the ground, 6 runs are scored.

## Law 20 — Dead Ball

The ball becomes dead when a batsman is dismissed, a boundary is scored, or the umpire calls dead ball. No runs can be scored from a dead ball.

## Law 21 — No-Ball

A no-ball is called for an illegal delivery (overstepping the crease, throwing, dangerous bowling). One run is awarded to the batting side and the delivery must be re-bowled. In limited-overs cricket, a free hit follows a no-ball.

## Law 22 — Wide Ball

A wide is called when the ball is bowled so far from the batsman that it cannot be hit by a normal stroke. One run is awarded and the delivery must be re-bowled.

## Law 23 — Bye and Leg Bye

A bye is a run scored when the ball does not touch the batsman or bat. A leg bye is scored when the ball hits the batsman's body (not the bat). Both are added to the team total, not the batsman's individual score.

## Law 24 — Fielder's Absence; Substitutes

A substitute fielder may replace a player who leaves the field. A substitute may not bat, bowl or captain without the opposing captain's consent.

## Law 25 — Batsman's Innings

A batsman's innings begins when they come out to bat and ends when they are dismissed, retire, or the innings closes.

## Law 26 — Practice on the Field

No practice is allowed on the pitch during the match. Warm-up throws are allowed on the outfield.

## Law 27 — The Wicket-Keeper

The wicket-keeper stands behind the stumps and may stump the batsman or take catches. The keeper may wear gloves with webbing between thumb and forefinger only.

## Law 28 — The Fielder

Any fielder may stop the ball with any part of the body. A fielder may not use clothing to field the ball.

## Law 29 — The Wickets Are Down

A wicket is down when a bail is removed from the stumps, or a stump is struck out of the ground by the ball, bat or fielder.

## Law 30 — Batsman Out of His Ground

A batsman is out of his ground when no part of his bat or body is grounded behind the popping crease. A run-out can occur when the wicket is put down while out of ground.

## Law 31 — Appeals

An appeal ("How's that?") must be made by the fielding side before the bowler begins the next delivery. The umpire gives the batsman out or not out.

## Law 32 — Bowled

A batsman is out bowled when his wicket is put down by a delivery from the bowler, without touching any fielder. Bowled takes precedence over most other dismissals.

## Law 33 — Timed Out

A new batsman must be ready to face the next delivery within 3 minutes of the previous dismissal. Failure results in a "timed out" dismissal.

## Law 34 — Hit the Ball Twice

A batsman may hit the ball twice only to guard his wicket. He is out if he intentionally hits it a second time for any other purpose.

## Law 35 — Hit Wicket

A batsman is out hit wicket if, while playing a shot, he dislodges the bails with his bat, body or helmet.

## Law 36 — Leg Before Wicket (LBW)

A batsman is out LBW if the ball hits any part of the batsman's body (not the hand holding the bat) in line with the wickets, and would have gone on to hit the stumps. The ball must pitch in line or on the off side.

## Law 37 — Obstructing the Field

A batsman is out if he obstructs a fielder attempting a run-out. The fielder must not wilfully obstruct the batsman.

## Law 38 — Run Out

A batsman is out run out if, while the ball is in play, he is out of his ground and the wicket at his end is fairly put down by the fielding side.

## Law 39 — Stumped

A batsman is out stumped if he steps beyond the popping crease while playing a shot (not attempting a run) and the wicket-keeper puts down the wicket.

## Law 40 — The Wicket-Keeper

The wicket-keeper must stand behind the stumps. If he moves in front before the ball passes the stumps, a no-ball is called.

## Law 41 — The Fielder

Fielders may not field the ball with their cap or clothing. Doing so results in 5 penalty runs to the batting side.

## Law 42 — Fair and Unfair Play

The umpires are the sole judges of fair and unfair play. Dangerous or unfair bowling, time-wasting and pitch tampering are penalized.

## Limited-Overs Rules

### One Day Internationals (ODI)

Each team faces 50 overs. A bowler may bowl a maximum of 10 overs per innings. Powerplay restrictions limit fielders outside the 30-yard circle during the first 10 overs.

### Twenty20 (T20)

Each team faces 20 overs. A bowler may bowl a maximum of 4 overs per innings. The powerplay lasts for the first 6 overs with only 2 fielders allowed outside the circle.

### Duckworth-Lewis-Stern Method

In rain-affected limited-overs matches, the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method calculates revised targets based on overs remaining and wickets in hand.

## Key Terminology

- **Maiden over**: An over in which no runs are scored.
- **Dot ball**: A delivery from which no runs are scored.
- **Yorker**: A delivery that pitches at the batsman's feet.
- **Bouncer**: A delivery that pitches short and rises towards the batsman's head.
- **Googly**: A leg-spinner's delivery that turns the opposite way.
- **Silly point**: A close catching position near the batsman on the off side.
- **Slip**: A fielding position behind the batsman on the off side.
- **Nightwatchman**: A lower-order batsman sent in late in the day to protect a better batsman.
- **Tail-ender**: A lower-order batsman who is not a strong batter.
- **All-rounder**: A player who is skilled at both batting and bowling.
- **Golden duck**: A batsman dismissed on the first ball they face.
- **Hat-trick**: A bowler taking three wickets with three consecutive deliveries.
